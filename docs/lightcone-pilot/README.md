# Lightcone Research Pilot

The Lightcone Research Pilot is an activity within [Agentic Research @ LSST DESC](../../README.md). Participating DESC members will test and help improve Lightcone Research's agentic tooling on real LSST science questions and workflows. The Lightcone Research team, made up of DESC members, supports this work with focused engineering, compute, and model inference.

The aim is to learn where the tooling is scientifically useful, identify its limitations, and use scientific feedback to improve it. Workflows, evaluation methods, and lessons from the pilot can help establish practices the wider Collaboration can reuse. The broader Agentic Research community also welcomes other tools, approaches, and initiatives.

**Launch status:** the call for ideas is planned for **12 October 2026** and remains unpublished pending approval. Propose ideas by **16 October 2026**. Participating projects will be proposed as sprints at **DESC Sprint Week**, where the Lightcone Research Pilot will kick off with DESC members.

## What participating involves

Because engineering, compute, and inference resources are limited and the goal is a focused effort with fast turnaround, the Lightcone team aims to extend dedicated support to a limited number of projects, subject to capacity, agreed scope, and approved access arrangements.

Everyone is welcome to try things, develop their own workflows, collaborate, and share lessons, whether or not their project receives dedicated support. [General](https://github.com/LSSTDESC/agentic-research/discussions/categories/general), [Show & Tell](https://github.com/LSSTDESC/agentic-research/discussions/categories/show-and-tell), and [Office Hours](../office-hours.md) are open ways to participate.

Scientific collaborators work hands-on with the Lightcone team: identify the question, help scope a useful test of the tooling, provide scientific context and appropriate test problems, and evaluate whether outputs are correct and useful. Regular feedback on scientific validity, usability, and failure cases helps the team improve the tools and iterate quickly; there is no arbitrary weekly-hour quota.

You do not need a fully specified solution, prior agentic AI experience, or familiarity with Lightcone's tools. Bring a meaningful scientific question, a tractable early milestone, a way to validate progress, and a willingness to try the tools and give feedback. Existing software or data can help but is not required at submission.

## From idea to participating project

**Proposal → discussion of fit and capacity → joint scoping and support agreement → sprint proposal → kickoff at DESC Sprint Week → review and next steps.**

The October 16 deadline gives the team and interested scientific collaborators a starting point for developing sprint proposals. DESC Sprint Week will be the setting for hands-on work together and the kickoff of the Lightcone Research Pilot. Specific sprint scope and participation arrangements will be worked out with the researchers involved.

Once the call opens, use one [Lightcone Research Pilot Proposals](https://github.com/LSSTDESC/agentic-research/discussions/categories/lightcone-research-pilot-proposals) Discussion per research idea for testing and improving Lightcone's tooling through this focused collaboration. This category is exclusively for participation in the Lightcone pilot and discussion of those proposals. Use [General](https://github.com/LSSTDESC/agentic-research/discussions/categories/general) for broader research ideas, potential collaborations, and other initiatives.

The form is intended to take about 10–15 minutes and asks for the scientific goal, a useful pilot outcome, and a scientific contact's involvement. Optional fields cover possible agent roles; links to repositories, analysis code, notebooks, and related work; data and access requirements; computing requirements such as running at NERSC, CC-IN2P3, or another environment; and related working groups. It is fine to be unsure how agents could help or not yet know the infrastructure details.

Read [Participating in the Lightcone Research Pilot](participation.md) for how the team works with interested researchers to identify useful projects within available capacity. Community discussion helps refine ideas; upvotes do not allocate support.

## Useful outcomes and scientific standards

Potential outcomes include improvements to Lightcone's tooling informed by scientific use, documented workflows, evaluation tasks, reproducible examples, and reusable tools where permissions allow. Record the provenance of data and generated artifacts, the tools and configurations used, validation checks, and limitations so scientific collaborators can inspect and reproduce the work. Scientific correctness requires domain review and evidence.

Unpublished research and DESC products are shared or released only with the necessary approvals. Follow [DESC policies](https://lsstdesc.org/pages/policies.html). This public repository is not a data-sharing authorization mechanism; keep restricted material and access-controlled links out of proposals and external inference services unless sharing is explicitly authorized through the appropriate process.

Projects participating in this activity will appear on [Lightcone Research Pilot Projects](projects.md). All DESC members are welcome at [Office Hours](../office-hours.md), and community experimentation can continue independently of this pilot.
